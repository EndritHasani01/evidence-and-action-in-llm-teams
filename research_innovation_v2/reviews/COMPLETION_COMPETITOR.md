# Independent review of the completion-reopening competitor

Review date: **4 October 2026**. This review starts from the current PS. It does not assume the September-30 recommendation is correct. The historical workspace is preserved unchanged.

## Verdict

**Do not replace the original PS with completion/search reopening on the present evidence.** The prior branch identified a real distinction: valid retained objects do not establish that enough of the right work has been performed. Its final mechanism remained an engineering synthesis with unexecuted efficacy and feasibility gates. The new literature makes that direction more crowded, while its required task/interface changes remain substantial.

This is a comparative project decision, not a claim that completion reopening is scientifically worthless. If a PS-preserving addition later fails, the completion proposal can still be tested on its own merits. It should not receive priority merely because it has an existing recommendation document.

## What the prior branch got right

An incumbent can remain feasible while becoming suboptimal after a requirement relaxation or a newly available option. Revalidating its positive facts cannot establish global search adequacy. The old branch's counterexamples expose this cleanly. Its strongest contribution is the separation between **artifact validity** and **adequacy of stopping**, with explicit acknowledgement that a receipt proves a process occurred rather than proving semantic completeness.

It also anticipated its own strongest reduction: a completion record can be represented as a node that reads the objective and domain-membership versions. Existing recovery machinery can then invalidate and recompute it. A root fallback avoids trusting absent scope notes, but frequently reduces to broad reconsideration with retained evidence. These admissions are valuable evidence and should be retained.

## Why the redirection has not earned replacement status

1. **It alters the scientific object.** The PS keeps every peer active for all four continuation rounds and does not authorize permanent `done`. Its central failure is retaining unhelpful derived work after source correction. To study omitted search, the proposed redirection adds query scopes, acquisition receipts, task obligations, variable search coverage and completion acceptance.
2. **It changes the main estimand.** The previous proposal replaces the reset-by-change interaction with enforced reopening versus generic reconsideration and weakens dissemination to a secondary question.
3. **It changes benchmark coverage.** Its recommendation removes the six-family main matrix in favor of three search-oriented families and an independent finite-search adapter. Prefix sums and difference arrays are especially useful controls for local/global semantic footprints, but are poorly matched to unperformed search.
4. **It needs positive-artifact semantics the PS does not supply.** A guarantee that expensive old artifacts are admissible needs current checks or structured contracts. Treating retained prose as certified would reintroduce the unresolved problem.
5. **Its favorable behavior has not been observed.** CPU examples prove logical possibility; they do not show natural omitted-work failures, useful scopes, or an advantage over broad reconsideration.

## Strongest possible version of the competitor

The most defensible completion study would ask whether **automatic revocation of stopping status** improves recovery beyond a fresh planner given identical preserved information and computational opportunity, when scope notes are incomplete. It would keep the outcome distinction between a still-valid incumbent, a changed optimum, and the work actually inspected. Its negative result would show that broad reconsideration or a reminder already suffices.

The minimum decisive experiment needs all of these:

- A natural shared prefix in a finite search problem with genuine acquisition choices.
- One authoritative opportunity change, an answer-preserving revision, and a sham.
- Generic reconsideration and enforced reopening with identical initial state, available evidence, call allowance, prompts except enforcement, and runtime validation capabilities.
- A matched-observation control to establish whether any advantage is merely mandatory additional acquisition.
- An established control/membership-aware implementation supplied the same observable metadata.
- At least one unlisted newly relevant option and one misleading scope note.

If the control-aware baseline executes the identical policy, the contribution becomes an application-level comparison. If refreshed observations erase the difference, describe acquisition enforcement as the cause; do not claim a superior reasoning representation. If the agent chooses no relevant search exclusions in natural prefixes, the benchmark is manufacturing the failure.

## Relative value against PS-preserving alternatives

| Criterion | Completion redirection | Focused PS-preserving boundary/measurement extension |
|---|---|---|
| Central question continuity | Retains general recovery motivation but shifts to search adequacy | Directly asks what to retain after the original collaboration |
| Existing six-task fit | Weak for at least some exact array tasks | Can use all six task-specific footprints and output placements |
| Extra semantic assumptions | Query coverage, obligations, admissible artifacts, completion discharge | Potentially only visible sources, actual prompt/message history and offline exact outcomes |
| Mechanism novelty | Weak as a general algorithm; interface/empirical claim possible | Also weak for filtering itself; strongest as a measured limit or decision-relevant characterization |
| Causal difficulty | Acquisition, extra work, scope quality and context rendering intertwine | Can be smaller, but token removal and communication feedback still need explicit controls |
| Implementation | New finite-search adapter, query wrapper, lifecycle, output rules | Trace analysis can be CPU-only; one diagnostic arm if discrimination remains |
| Reuse of main PS evidence | Partial; main matrix/estimand replaced | All planned main branches and controls remain useful |
| Useful failure | Reminder/fresh planning suffices | Runtime selectivity collapses, simple reset captures value, or exposure fails to predict utility |

This is a conditional comparison. A poorly identified exposure metric is not stronger merely because it is cheap. The focused extension must deliver an interpretable quantity, a demonstrable distinction from known systems, and falsifiable predictions. The completion competitor wins only if those fail and its own natural-prefix/acquisition gates pass.

## Cost and feasibility comparison

The historical completion plan proposes **5,592 calls for 36 worlds** or **4,032 for 24**, before its optional scoped policy. Its 24-main-GPU-hour break-even averages are about **15.45 s/call** and **21.43 s/call**. These are schedule arithmetic, not measured throughput. It fits on paper partly by using fewer worlds and families than the PS's 48/36 worlds; that is a scientific tradeoff, not a free innovation.

An observational analysis of existing PS traces adds no model calls. One extra four-round branch adds 16 calls per selected prefix: 192 calls on 12 worlds or 384 on 24. A second matched branch doubles this increment. At the PS's illustrative 15 allocated seconds/call, 384 calls consume 1.6 aggregate GPU-hours before overhead. These figures are capacity calculations only; actual timing and the fixed reserve still govern admission.

The completion runtime is plausible for a bachelor's project with more than a month, but it has more unvalidated dependencies. A simple boundary study that uses the PS's already planned instrumentation has a better expected value unless it is scientifically vacuous. That is the burden the final novelty and falsification reviews must settle.

## Recommendation to the main investigation

Keep completion reopening as a **ranked competitor and optional later transfer study**. Preserve its counterexamples, search-sufficiency distinction, strong baselines and failure gates. Do not port its obligation lifecycle into the PS simply to add visible machinery. First examine whether the PS already contains a sharper phenomenon about recovery after actual information sharing that the single-hop, structured-DAG and return-memory predecessors do not answer.
