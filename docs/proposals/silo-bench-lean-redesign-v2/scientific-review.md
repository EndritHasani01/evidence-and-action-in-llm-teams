# Scientific review agenda before adoption

**OPEN REVIEW QUESTIONS — PROPOSAL ONLY**

These questions expose the weak points of the [v2 proposal](../silo-bench-lean-redesign-v2.md). They are not extra experimental cells, new gates, or silently adopted design changes. Resolve conceptual questions before implementation; resolve empirical ones through the bounded qualification and pilot only after authorization.

## Questions that could change the design

| Question | Why it matters | Smallest useful way to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Is the retained-work package the intended estimand? | Equal canonical sources do not equal equal information; old summaries carry facts and calculations | Review explicit example views and state the claim in ordinary language |
| Do native-size tasks produce useful historical work? | Empty history or immediate raw centralization can make the intended mechanism weak | Inspect complete pilot prefixes; allow actual strategies rather than forcing a preferred one |
| Is six turns enough for natural completion? | Last-turn review debt can determine outcomes more than recovery reasoning | Examine legal scripted traces, then pilot requests, replies, and terminal states |
| Is the conditional edit population acceptable? | Eligible answer-changing edits are not arbitrary production updates | Review the sampler and disclose its conditioning; keep selection metadata hidden from peers |
| Does the chosen effort measure answer the practical question? | Extra context and thinking can cost more even when call counts tie | Pair success with ready-model inference time and its token components |
| Is a 12-world exploratory result adequate for the academic goal? | Course evidence and a substantial empirical paper need different scope | Agree on claim level before committing compute |

## Information boundaries worth walking through on paper

### A changed record was previously forwarded

The owner receives its current value and a before/after receipt. Another peer that held the old authoritative record learns that its version is obsolete, but does not automatically receive the replacement payload. Matched retain/reset siblings must have identical current/pending source status. Old prose is not silently rewritten into a correct message.

Review whether the proposed renderer makes source authority understandable without revealing hidden values or treating historical claims as current evidence. An implementation that marks a record obsolete but leaves it in the current-source table would undermine the comparison.

### The history contains a useful summary but no attached records

Retain may know a partner's local set through prose; reset may not. This is allowed by the package intervention. Do not call it an accidental leak, and do not “repair” reset by reconstructing that set in a host-generated source table. Such a repair would change the treatment.

### Both agents submitted, but one sends a final message

A new message creates review work and can invalidate an active submission. The proposal intentionally avoids a semantic host classifier deciding that the message is harmless. Review the action ordering and terminal-state meaning carefully: no pending review may be ignored merely to label the episode complete.

If this creates frequent cap failures, the issue is the adopted lifecycle/horizon combination. Increasing the horizon is a scientific and budget decision; automatically accepting an old submission is not an equivalent optimization.

### The original collaboration failed

All mathematically valid main worlds remain in the all-world analysis. A correct-prefix stratum supports the narrower recovery claim, with its actual denominator visible. Never replace failed prefixes or keep sampling until twelve successful collaborations exist under the original allocation.

### The agents could infer that every edit changes the answer

The main sampler intentionally selects expansions or contractions. Revealing that fact can eliminate missing-support uncertainty. Keep edit-class labels, expected deltas, and eligibility rules out of peer instructions. Public study documentation may describe them; agent prompts must not leak them.

## Reading equal-accuracy results honestly

| Observed pattern | Potentially useful conclusion | Claim to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Both policies accurate, retain uses less inference | Retained work can save measured effort in this regime | General superiority or a hidden accuracy benefit |
| Both accurate, retain costs more | Longer history may impose net cost under this interface | Extra context is always harmful |
| Both accurate with similar effort | No resolved practical difference in the observed sample | Statistical equivalence or proof history is unnecessary |
| Both fail, full-information controls succeed | Distributed evidence, communication, horizon, or recovery may be the bottleneck | Pure anchoring without trace evidence |
| Both fail, full-information controls also fail | Basic competence limits interpretation of recovery | A meaningful test of memory policies has necessarily occurred |
| Revised reset helps, sham reset hurts | A correction-specific pattern may exist alongside ordinary reset costs | The interaction alone proves reset is best overall |
| Agents centralize raw sources | The allowed strategy reduces the need for distributed summary reuse | Invalidating those worlds or forbidding centralization after seeing it |

Effort comparisons should include all outcomes. A fast wrong answer is not an efficient solution. Restricting cost analysis to successful branches creates post-treatment selection; show the full paired outcome categories first.

## Qualification concerns to resolve without outcome fishing

The complete proposal's gates remain the reference: at least 11/12 exact full-information answers per eligible mode with no malformed/truncated final actions; selected-mode hardware checks; then two complete pilot worlds with correct original collaboration, correct full-information controls, usable history, and no unresolved protocol defects.

These are small screens, not population-reliability estimates. Passing both pilot worlds is encouraging but does not guarantee main-prefix success. Conversely, demanding a nonzero or preferred treatment effect during the pilot would bias the research direction. Model and protocol selection must depend on competence, usability, and cost—not the sign of retain/reset results.

If prompts, limits, task size, or model configuration change after a failed screen, disclose the development decision and account for requalification. “Still under the main budget” does not make unplanned search scientifically free.

## What not to resolve by adding machinery

- Do not add a supervisor model to identify when peers have enough evidence; that creates an unbudgeted, potentially informative intervention.
- Do not add a semantic history extractor merely to equalize contexts; that changes the retained object and introduces another fallible process.
- Do not add many controls to isolate every mechanism while retaining twelve worlds. Prefer a narrower claim or a larger explicit budget.
- Do not use repeated seeds or additional bootstrap draws as substitutes for independent worlds.
- Do not make correctness trigger an extra turn, a retry, or early stopping in live execution.

## Review outcome to record later

A useful adoption review should resolve three issues in writing: the acceptable claim, the permitted lifecycle, and the conditions under which qualification would stop. Unresolved empirical uncertainty is normal; ambiguity about what the intervention means is not. If the intended claim requires pure anchoring, broad generality, or reliable small-effect detection, this proposal should be revised before implementation rather than relabeled afterward.
